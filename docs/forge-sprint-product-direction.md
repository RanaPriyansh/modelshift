# Forge product direction

Updated: 2026-07-29

## Chosen direction

**Forge helps a student build one useful thing in seven days and leave with honest, inspectable proof of the work.**

The initial wedge is university students in computing, product, design, research, and maker communities who already want to create something but struggle to scope, finish, and explain it. The product is a browser-local sprint workbench—not an AI tutor, generic task manager, social feed, or credential.

Job to be done:

> When I have an idea that could help my portfolio, class, club, or community, help me make it small enough to ship, keep moving for one week, and explain what is genuinely mine.

## Options considered

| Direction | Immediate pull | Structural weakness | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic AI study companion | Familiar and easy to demo | Crowded; rewards answers and chat volume rather than capability | Do not lead with it |
| Personal productivity planner | Broad audience | Weak differentiation and weak proof of a useful outcome | Reject as the wedge |
| Cohort/accountability network | Social motivation | Cold start, moderation, and performative activity | Later only if groups ask |
| Adaptive learning Worlds | Strong pedagogy and existing implementation | Creates a mode shift before there is a concrete reason to study | Keep as optional Labs |
| Seven-day build-to-proof sprint | Concrete outcome, bounded scope, portfolio value, and a traveling artifact | Must earn trust through good templates and real completions | **Build now** |

## Product principles

1. **Artifact before activity.** Completion means something useful exists—not points, messages, or streaks.
2. **One audience and one finish line.** The system forces scope because ambitious ambiguity is the primary opponent.
3. **Proof without surveillance theater.** Evidence and declarations are inspectable and scoped. Forge does not claim to verify identity, mastery, or authorship.
4. **Help leaves before proof.** Day 6 protects a short no-generative-AI pass: explain the core flow, make a change, and check reality.
5. **Local first.** No account is required and private sprint data stays in the browser. Export is explicit.
6. **Projects stay primary.** Existing learning Worlds remain optional Labs when a project exposes a real gap.
7. **No attention traps.** No feed, score, leaderboard, streak, notification pressure, or infinite backlog.

## The seven-day contract

1. Define and scope
2. Meet the user or problem
3. Build the smallest end-to-end core
4. Test and repair the biggest failure
5. Polish the important path
6. Run the protected Proof Lab
7. Ship, reflect, and state open questions

The daily unit is a meaningful move plus the decision it changed. Final proof combines the finish line, shipped artifacts, evidence links, daily decisions, protected proof, reflection, and honest open edges.

## Game theory and growth

Forge should make the behavior that benefits the learner also benefit the network.

1. A learner starts from a recognizable project pattern.
2. The learner ships an artifact worth showing.
3. Exported proof makes the process legible to a peer, teacher, recruiter, or collaborator.
4. A viewer can reuse the pattern—not copy the learner’s answers—to start a new sprint.
5. Completed examples reveal which templates produce useful outcomes.

This loop distributes through the work itself. It does not require contact uploads, spam invitations, public rankings, or a feed.

Aligned incentives:

- Learners win by narrowing scope and finishing.
- Viewers win by quickly understanding the artifact and the judgment behind it.
- Educators win by seeing process evidence without adopting surveillance.
- Forge wins when useful projects are completed and proven patterns are reused.

Failure modes:

- Prestige scores make users optimize presentation over learning.
- Template rankings encourage broad, shallow projects.
- Hidden AI completion lets polished outputs crowd out genuine capability.
- Mandatory accounts hurt privacy and activation.
- A feed makes attention—not finished work—the scarce resource.

## Product-market-fit hypotheses

The first strong segment is not “all students.” It is students with an imminent reason to show work:

- internship or placement preparation;
- hackathons and club projects;
- capstones and independent studies;
- portfolio rebuilds;
- student organizers solving a repeated campus problem.

Primary activation: **a valid sprint is created and the first daily move is saved.**

North-star outcome: **a useful artifact ships with an inspectable proof record.**

Supporting measures:

- setup-to-first-save conversion;
- Day 1 to Day 3 continuation;
- Day 3 to shipped-proof completion;
- time to first evidence link;
- completed proofs opened or exported;
- “meaningfully disappointed without Forge” among people who completed a sprint;
- evidence that a viewer understood the learner’s contribution faster.

These are hypotheses, not traction claims. The browser-local MVP intentionally has no analytics. Initial evidence should come from consented pilots and interviews.

## Validation plan

1. Recruit 12–20 students across two high-intent contexts, such as an internship club and a hackathon cohort.
2. Ask each participant to bring a real project; do not supply a classroom exercise.
3. Observe setup and Day 1 without coaching. Record where scope fails.
4. Check in after Days 3 and 7. Inspect the artifact and proof, not self-reported busyness.
5. Interview at least one proof viewer per completed sprint.
6. Improve or remove templates based on completion quality.
7. Add optional sync or public sharing only after people repeatedly work around the local boundary.

A promising signal is repeated, unprompted evidence that students finish narrower projects, explain their decisions, and voluntarily show the proof to someone who matters.

## Current boundary

The MVP includes a project-first homepage, idea intake, four templates, safe browser-local multi-sprint storage, a seven-day workbench, evidence links, explicit AI-use boundaries, a protected Day 6 Proof Lab, exportable learner-declared proof, My Sprints, Templates, and optional Labs. The previous learning home remains at /learn.

It excludes accounts, cloud sync, public profiles, collaboration, automated verification, scoring, payments, and a recommendation feed. Those are possible later layers, not prerequisites for learning whether the build-to-proof loop is loved.
